## A Thought Experiment or Two

The concept of monks has fascinated me to no end. There are these robed, jacked guys (or gals![^1]) who do nothing but work out and meditate. No wives, no kids, no jobs, no YouTube, no Clash Royale.

Motivated by their way of life, I considered this thought experiment:

1. You could never have a romantic relationship in the future.  
2. After being informed of this, you can choose to continue living or die painlessly.

Most people (whether they admit it or not) would choose to continue living. Maybe with even more passion and drive towards their friends, family, and other goals. Fewer options can actually increase satisfaction because they pull us away from the constant tug-of-war of desire.

To be clear, I am not suggesting that you shave your head and swear off sexual relations forever. This is just a proof of concept: life is still worth living, even in the unlikely "worst case."

A similar experiment would be to imagine that you're a heterosexual man in a world where women never existed (bear with me). In this hypothetical, you'd have nothing to miss and you’d aspire to live your best life without being moopy from lacking a partner. Maybe, just maybe, you'd even listen to love songs and think about yourself instead of the one that got away.

Here's the point: ideally you would be happy without a relationship. If you have a deep-seated desire to form a bond and perhaps to have a family, that's beautiful. But that shouldn't be bringing you great anguish. _Ideally_, it would sting about as much as failing an interview or bombing an exam.

## Crushing ANTs

Imagine scrolling reels or tiktok with no cares in the world before being jump scared by an image of slenderman... Now let's do it again. Except you're not scrolling reels and you're actually trying to sleep before your big presentation tomorrow. And instead of slenderman, you suddenly remember your ex and wonder _"Why wasn't I good enough?"_. 

That is an Automatic Negative Thought or Ant for short.

If your desire for love is strong and unmet, it’s easy to start linking that absence with your worst fears about yourself. That’s where **crushing Ants** becomes essential.

Ants are disempowering. Worse they’re addictive. Instead of picking ourselves up when we’re down, we spiral. We reinforce the pain. Anything that isn't self-love becomes self-hate.

The ability to acknowledge an Ant is one definition for mindfulness. Mindfulness is a whole other can of worms about interpretting our emotions as stimuli that call for some action blah blah blah blah(it's actually really cool!).[^2]

---

### So how do we kill an ANT?

As fast as it appeared is as fast as we cut it down. But we don’t stop there. We **replace** the thought with its opposite.

- *"I'm never going to be the one for her and I'm pathetic for trying again and again."*  
  → *"Just because she isn't the one for me doesn't mean no one is. I'm learning, growing, and moving forward."*

- *"I'm fat, autistic, have no friends and will never find love."*  
  → *"I may not have the connections I want yet, but I'm capable of creating deep, meaningful relationships. I am lovable. And I will be the first to love me."*

- *"Why can't I be normal?"*  
  → *"My struggles are real, but they don't make me less. I am human. I am allowed to be me."*

These are real thoughts sampled from r/kitchencels. [Check the footnote for their associated foods!][^3]

The ability to rewrite our narrative is very real. ANTs often sound absurd when said aloud — they’re the things we only tell ourselves when we’re most vulnerable. If a friend came to you with these thoughts, you’d never say, *“You’re right, give up.”*

So don’t say it to yourself.

## Closing Thoughts

When you approach life from a base of happiness, the hurdles of life stay just that: hurdles. Not catastrophes. Not identity crises. Just hurdles.

It’s okay to be alone. It’s okay to feel lonely.  
Just don’t let those thoughts hijack your story.

Live, breathe, and be happy.  
There'll be time to talk about dating after that.
