Science based lifting has arisen as a momentus subculture in the fitness community that seeks to replace hype and aura with t-tests and randomized controlled trials. 

In an ideal world, my case would be closed here.
Unfortunately, we do not live in that universe. 

At the forefront of the movement are Dr. Mike Israetel and Jeff Nippard. Both of whom have been recently embroiled in controversy. But here's the part that everyone misses: what they discussed was never science to begin with. Science is the accumulation of data and methods to support a model of understanding. 

Exercise "science" fails spectacularly at this. Most studies are brutally underfunded and consequently lack the sample sizes needed to produce significant results. In fact, these tiny studies amount to a ceremonial rain dance to summon an exercise science doctorate degree from Mt. Olympus. I claim this because when we try to probe for impact within small samples, we are essentially hunting for large effect sizes.

<p style="text-align: center;">....</p>

But... this is exactly what we do on an individual basis when we rotate in different exercises, supplements or other habits. So, in many cases, these studies are essentially equivalent to the anecdote of someone who tried a bunch of shit(pardon me).

Think back to when you first started a sport or lifting or any physical activity. You didn't dissect peer reviewed papers at 3 am to conclude that lifting builds muscle. The effect size is so massive that we saw loved ones, people on TV and people on social media who transformed their bodies in relatively short spans of time from working out. 

That was all the convincing most people needed. And sure enough, many of us started our journies and found ourselves waking up to bigger biceps after a matter of months. 

What I mean to say here is that you are not delusional for relying on anecdotes. The cost of trying a variety of things within the realm of exercise is minimal so you are effectively free to find the exercises or routine that drives a perceived larger effect for you.

***But Christian... is there any nuance to this chaotic, misbegotten world of exercise science?***

Yea, unfortunately. 

Exercise science is a broad catch all term that is the equivalent of a shiny pokemon in the eyes of bright-eyed young men who want like lifting and also want to go to college(ie, it's probably a term that got popularized by some random major at some random university. Don't quote me on this though). 

That said, it encompasses things like rehabilitation or exercise physiology which are legitimate and vitally important domains. Injury management is not an area where we can feel around in the dark and hope to find the right solution. We need ***drumroll*** SCIENCE!

Science is the dull claymore that we will use to hack apart the barbarian that is injury management. The cost of failing to rehab a knee in an older patient could literally be their life [^1]. 

As a consequence, it literally becomes necessary to approach this with science. Unfortunately, as it stands, it is still difficult to fund(think several million dollars) and perform longitudinal studies on various exercise based interventions on people with injury. A lot of existing knowledge and methods lie with experts at the time of my writing. 

Bringing this back to Dr. Mike and Jeff Nippard, I believe that their bastardization of science is plainly terrible. At the same time, they and other content creators have done a good job at attempting to make exercise more accessible for the elderly and folks with chronic injury. I think that's the area where current science based lifters actually shine despite it almost never being under the purview of social media. 

**The Ultimate Takeaway**: it's okay if you are not optimizing your workouts via science... because neither are they.
